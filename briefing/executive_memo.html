1Purpose

This memo turns the Operating Intelligence portal into one decision document. It answers three questions for the PRG. Where does each platform actually stand on the public record? Which levers move EBITDA and the exit multiple fastest? What should the PRG do in the next 90 days? The benchmark is our own first exit. Smith + Howard went from ~100 to ~800 professionals, 11 offices, nine add-ons and ~4x revenue in about 3.5 years and sold to TPG Growth in August 2026.28 Revenue, EBITDA, leverage and value figures are outside-in estimates (est.) built from PPP loans, Form D and Form ADV filings, BDC schedules, franchise disclosure documents (FDDs) and peer ratios. Each should be replaced with company actuals in the first 30 days.

Bottom line
  • The six platforms total roughly $540–750M of revenue and $66–101M of EBITDA (est., sum of ranges below). Two are already past the Smith + Howard hold: Thomas Scientific at 4.7 years and Fair Harbor at 4.5.
  • Thomas Scientific is the urgent item. Lenders mark the first-lien loan at 88.3% of par, the sponsor common is marked at about zero and the loan matures in December 2027.19 It needs a cash-and-refinancing plan before a growth plan.
  • The largest upside is in CET and Punctual Pros. CET has open space in New England and a funded wastewater pipeline. Punctual Pros can buy tuck-ins at ~6–7x and assemble a platform that sells in the 10x tier.25
  • The OS program (six operating systems and 45 AI agents) needs an est. $6.0–12.4M of investment. For that, the evidence supports +0.5–2.5 turns of exit multiple per platform, and the agents add ~$12.7M a year of run-rate value (est.).7,26

6The OS program and the tech-enabled multiple case

The case. Buyers pay for predictable, recurring and scalable earnings. PKF prices project-heavy, low-retention HVAC firms at 5–6x and repeatable-service firms with in-house capabilities at 10x+. Capstone/IMAP expect a ~3.0-turn spread between typical (6.8x) and premium (9.8x) middle-market deals. Scaled membership platforms print higher still: Champions ~18.5x, Sila ~17x. No source isolates a pure "technology premium"; the observed premiums bundle scale, recurring mix, margin and growth. Tech enablement can credibly claim ~0.5–2.5 turns. Each OS assembles proven vendors (ServiceTitan, Procore, ConnectWise/Kaseya, Shopify/Klaviyo) with a thin Broad Sky data layer, not custom software.7

8Competitive landscape

  • Broad Sky is small on this map. The flagship fund reports $335M sold (Form D), against $0.9–2.1B flagships at Trivest, Sterling and Gridiron. We win on co-invest and operating credibility, and Smith + Howard is the proof point for Fund II.12,28
  • Residential services is the hardest field. National consolidators set add-on pricing, and Sila, Legacy/HL Bowman, Ally and Northwinds are buying in or next to PP's counties. We should lean on the franchise-system angle and sub-$3M-EBITDA shops that large platforms skip.
  • CET has open space. Kohlberg's (RESA Power, Loenbro, CLEAResult) and Huron's (Criticore, Albireo) platforms are mostly outside New England. Watch Albireo's Upstate NY push and PEC of Connecticut's $61.1M Alaris deal (2025).6
  • No sponsor-backed platform copies Frontline's legal IT + billing model. The likely challengers are Alpine's Evergreen, EagleTree/Integreon and BV. Lock in law-firm MSP and billing add-ons before a generalist MSP builds a legal vertical.
  • Thomas Scientific faces one aggressive consolidator. Calibre Scientific (StoneCalibre) made 12+ lab distribution deals in 2024, mostly in Europe, which leaves U.S. line-card targets open.
  • Exit demand is deep. Shamrock bought Penta in 2025, so mid-market public affairs is uncrowded for BPI. Upmarket buyers (LGP, Goldman Sachs, Court Square, New Mountain, Bain, TPG) keep paying for scaled services platforms. Coverage of apparel sponsors for Fair Harbor is incomplete.12

Caveats

  • Every revenue, EBITDA, leverage and EV figure for a private company is an outside-in estimate (est.), mostly low confidence. None comes from management, and no material non-public information was used.
  • The KPI roadmaps (months 0–36) and OS and agent values are analyst assumptions for discussion, not forecasts or company guidance. Agent values are not de-duplicated against the OS ranges.
  • Fund size conflict. One brief cites a ~$137M Fund I closed in December 2024. SEC data show that $136.95M was raised by BSP-FL LP, the Frontline deal vehicle, while Broad Sky Partners, LP reports $335M sold (Form D/A, Apr 2025). This memo uses $335M.28
  • No public filing shows the debt of CET, BPI or Fair Harbor, so their leverage is inferred from sector norms. Web-search budgets ran out during several pulls, and paywalled sources (PitchBook, 9fin, Debtwire) were not accessed.
  • Vendor performance claims (ServiceTitan, Rilla, XOi, Togal, PermitFlow) are self-reported. The multiple-premium evidence bundles scale and recurring mix with technology. This memo is not investment advice.
