What this is

This memo reviews BSP's six portfolio companies using public records only. It shows where each company stands, where profit can grow fastest and what to do in the first 90 days. Figures not found in public filings are analyst estimates, marked est., to be checked against company actuals.

Terms: EBITDA is operating profit before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization. EV is enterprise value. A “turn” is one times EBITDA added to the sale price. DSO (days sales outstanding) is how long customers take to pay. Each OS (operating system) is a bundle of software and AI tools for one company. PP is Punctual Pros.

1Purpose

Where each company stands, which levers move EBITDA fastest, and what the PRG should do in 90 days. Benchmark: Smith + Howard, ~4x revenue in 3.7 years, sold to TPG Growth.28

Summary
  • Six companies: est. $540–750M revenue and $66–101M EBITDA.
  • Thomas Scientific refinancing plan: start the workstream now for the December 2027 maturity; grow EBITDA toward ~$42.5M to reach ~6x leverage.19,20
  • Largest upside: CET in New England wastewater; Punctual Pros through 6–7x tuck-ins.25
  • OS program: est. $6.0–12.4M for +0.5–2.5 turns per company.7,26

11Competitive landscape

  • BSP is small: $335M flagship versus $0.9–2.1B at Trivest, Sterling, Gridiron.12,28
  • Residential services is the hardest field; target shops consolidators skip.
  • CET has open space: Kohlberg and Huron companies sit mostly outside New England.6
  • No sponsor-backed rival copies Frontline's legal IT plus billing model.
  • Calibre Scientific made 12+ lab deals in 2024, mostly in Europe.
  • Exit demand is deep: LGP, Goldman Sachs and TPG keep buying services.12
