# Broad Sky OS: launch film

Draft 2 · 23 November 2026 · target length 4:30 · neutral narrator, third person

This is the script, the shot list and the production plan for a film that introduces Broad Sky Partners and
Broad Sky OS. Scenery and people are made with AI video generation. Every shot of the product is a real screen
recording. Draft 1 was written before most of the product existed. Draft 2 is the same film, rewritten around
what the product now does.

---

## 1. The idea in one line

The heat fails in a county building before dawn. By the time the van arrives, the technician knows the
building. The film then climbs, level by level, from the field to the firm. At the top, a partner types one
line and the globe flies to three companies.

The story runs **from the field up to the firm**. That order shows the levels without explaining them. The
"Viewing as" switch is the hinge, shown once.

## 2. Rules for this film

Kept from draft 1:

1. **Every product shot is a real screen recording** of Broad Sky OS. AI video cannot draw readable screens.
2. **All words, numbers and names on screen are added in the edit**, never generated.
3. **No generated likeness of any real person, and no generated logo.** People are invented and unnamed.
   Uniforms, vans and buildings carry no marks.
4. **Vendor names appear only where the product itself shows them**, never as a claim. The film never implies
   a partnership or that a company uses a product.
5. **Nobody at the firm is addressed by name.** The narrator speaks about the firm, not to a person.
6. **Only numbers that are on the product on the day of the final cut.** Every one is listed in section 8
   and must be re-checked before the voice is recorded.
7. **A closing card says the film uses AI-generated footage.**

Added in draft 2:

8. The words "playbook" and "sandbox" are not used, spoken or written. The film never says or implies that
   the firm or a company uses a named vendor's product.
9. The globe shots are recordings of the product's own Globe on its key-free imagery (US Geological Survey
   imagery, which is public domain, with open terrain and OpenStreetMap names). No third party's recording
   limit applies, but the map's attribution line stays in frame in every globe shot. If the photorealistic
   imagery is ever recorded instead, the limit in the research note applies: one clip of 30 seconds or less,
   labelled "For promotional purposes only", with the map provider's logo and attribution in frame and no
   street-level imagery. This script records none of it.
10. The address typed on screen is a **public building**: the York County Judicial Center, 45 N George St,
    York. Never a private home. Its inside is visibly dashed and labelled "Likely". The film never claims to
    know the inside of a home.
11. No line about hiring anyone, or about money lost by not hiring anyone. The closing card credits the maker
    plainly.
12. Generated footage shows invented, unnamed people from behind or at a distance, no logos, no readable text.
    Generated buildings are invented. A place name never sits over a generated building.

## 4. Narration (the full voice-over)

Read slowly. Short sentences. No emphasis on brand names. Pauses marked "( )" are a full beat.

**Cold open** (0:00 to 0:30)

> Before dawn, in central Pennsylvania, the heat has gone out in a county building.
>
> A call is made. A van leaves the yard.
>
> And before the technician reaches the door, he knows the building. Where it stands. How big it is. When it
> was built. Where the furnace most likely sits.
>
> He did not search for any of it.
>
> He typed one sentence.

**The problem** (0:30 to 1:00)

> That technician works for a company owned by Broad Sky Partners, an investment firm in New York.
>
> Broad Sky owns six companies. They heat homes, wire buildings, supply laboratories, support law firms, run
> campaigns and make clothes.
>
> Each runs on its own software. Each keeps its own numbers. ( )
>
> And what one company learns does not easily reach the next.

**Title** (1:00 to 1:10)

> This is Broad Sky OS.
>
> One operating system, for the firm and every company it owns.

**The field** (1:10 to 2:10)

> It starts with the person doing the work.
>
> A technician's home holds today's jobs, and one box for a question. He types where he is going, in his own
> words.
>
> In a few seconds, the building is found. Its real outline, from imagery. The year it was built and its size,
> from public records, each with its source.
>
> Inside, the rooms are drawn dashed, and marked likely. The system says what it knows, and what it is
> guessing.
>
> The building turns in three dimensions, and can be downloaded. One more press, and the globe steps down to
> the street.
>
> Owner names are never shown.

**The hinge** (2:10 to 2:40)

> Everyone in Broad Sky OS is viewing it as someone.
>
> Forty-four roles, in five levels. The field. The office. Each company's leadership. The firm's team. The
> firm's leaders.
>
> Choose a role, and the menu and the home change with it. ( )
>
> Each home is built for one job. A technician sees today's jobs. A partner sees the firm.

**The company** (2:40 to 3:05)

> And every home opens with one question.
>
> For a head of operations, it is which hubs face the coldest nights this week.
>
> One press. The answer arrives as a picture, from the public forecast, with a note to the team already drafted.
>
> It waits as a draft. A person decides.

**The firm** (3:05 to 3:50)

> At the top, the managing partner.
>
> Six companies in one table. What each is estimated to earn, how long it has been held, and what moves it.
> Then today's items, and what is waiting for approval.
>
> One running figure, for strategy and operations, marked as an estimate.
>
> Then one line, typed in plain words. ( )
>
> The globe turns, and flies to three companies, one after another. Each with its fit, its size, and the reason
> it was chosen.
>
> The globe runs on free public imagery. Add the firm's own map key, and it turns photorealistic.

**Connections** (3:50 to 4:08)

> Broad Sky OS does not replace the software these companies run. It connects to it.
>
> One screen names every tool, and says plainly where each one stands. Live. Available. Planned.
>
> None is required to begin.

**Close** (4:08 to 4:30)

> Everything in this film was built without access to a single internal system.
>
> Public records, where they exist. Example data, clearly marked, where they do not.
>
> Connect the real systems, and the examples become the company's own records.
>
> The technician still types one sentence. The partner still types one line.
>
> Broad Sky OS.

**Closing card (text only, no voice):** Broad Sky OS · Made by Syed Rahman · the link · "A demonstration built
from public information. This film uses AI-generated footage. Product shots are screen recordings of Broad Sky
OS." · Map credits as the product shows them: imagery USGS The National Map; terrain AWS Terrain Tiles; map data
© OpenStreetMap contributors, © OpenMapTiles, OpenFreeMap.

## 6. Style guide (lock this before generating anything)

- **Look:** naturalistic, 35 mm feel, shallow depth of field, soft grain. Cold blue before dawn, warming through
  the film; the last third is golden. No lens flares, no neon, no "tech" glow.
- **People:** invented, ordinary, working adults. Seen from behind, in silhouette, or at middle distance. No
  close-ups of faces or hands. Plain navy and grey workwear, no logos.
- **Places:** real kinds of place, not real addresses. An old brick civic building, a basement plant room, a
  depot, an office, a lab, a beach town. No generated building stands in for the York County Judicial Center.
- **Camera:** one slow move per shot. Push in, pull back, or track. Nothing handheld after the cold open.
- **Screens:** as in section 5. Recorded speed is kept for typing; flights and the 3D turn may be slowed to 80
  percent. The globe's attribution line is never cropped.
- **Type:** the product's own typefaces. White on black for cards; one size for captions.
- **Sound:** real room tone under every scene. Music enters at the title, thins under the hinge, drops out for
  the first line of the close, and returns for the last. One motif, piano and low strings. No risers, no
  whooshes. The globe flight in shot 30 plays with music only.
- **Voice:** calm, mid-pitch, unhurried, American. If an AI voice is used, slow it to about 135 words a minute
  and add pauses by hand.

## 10. What is not yet

1. **Photorealistic imagery.** It needs the firm's own map key in the site's settings file, and only the owner
   enters a key. Not recorded in this film. If it is ever recorded: one clip of 30 seconds or less, labelled
   "For promotional purposes only", provider logo and attribution in frame, no street-level imagery. Whether a
   film like this counts as promotional is not confirmed; ask the provider first.
2. **House rooms inside a public building.** For the Judicial Center the likely layout is the house rule set:
   "Living room", "Bedroom 1", "Two stories over a full basement (likely)". On a 120,389 sq ft courthouse that
   reads wrong to a viewer. Decide before recording: either the product gets a layout rule for large
   non-residential buildings, or shot 15 is framed on the outline, the dashed walls and the "Likely" chip with the
   room labels too small to read.
3. **Vendor names on draft cards.** Draft cards carry a chip with an app name (for the VP operations draft,
   "Microsoft Teams"). That conflicts with rule 8. Relabel the chip in the product before recording, or frame it
   out in shot 26 (the script assumes framing).

   example homes ("Show me 418 Wexcombe Ln before I get there."). They are visible in shots 6, 14 and 21. Keep

   backend." Do a live dry run of the exact sentence on the recording day; the "no heat" sentence was tested
