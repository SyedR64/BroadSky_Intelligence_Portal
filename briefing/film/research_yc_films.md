Every beat timing, cuts-per-minute figure and "typical" number below is either sourced to a page that states it, or marked JUDGMENT (my inference, no source). Lengths for most videos are not published on the pages I could reach, so I say "not stated" rather than guess.

## 2. Common structure, beat by beat

What sources actually say:
- Hook first, product second, ask last. About 3 min or less. https://www.flowjam.com/blog/yc-startup-launch-video-2025-watch-steal-ship-faster
- Founder on camera in the first 5 seconds is the top-decile pattern (Flowjam, self-reported). 48% of tracked launches are founder-led; about 80% of the top 10%. https://www.flowjam.com/blog/state-of-launch-videos-2026
- Spend the first 10 to 20% of runtime on identity, context and stakes, then give the rest to the demonstration. https://www.sean-weldon.com/blog/2026-10-25-this-is-how-you-should-actually-make-launch-videos
- Shortcut had the founder on screen about 15 seconds before the demo. https://speedrun.substack.com/p/how-to-make-a-viral-launch-video
- Kite: one sentence, a demo, a second sentence. Adapt: name the use case in one line, then show three real tasks. https://www.flowjam.com/blog/product-launch-video-examples-2026
- Strong videos follow a clear narrative rather than a feature walkthrough. Hook, then product explanation. https://www.motiontheagency.com/blog/how-startups-use-demo-videos
- Name one product, one outcome, one promise. https://www.flowjam.com/blog/state-of-launch-videos-2026

Typical 2 minute beat map. JUDGMENT, assembled from the sources above; no source gives per-beat timings:

| Beat | Share of runtime | At 120 s |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open / hook | 0 to 5 s | 0:00 to 0:05 |
| Problem or stakes | 10 to 15% | 0:05 to 0:20 |
| Reveal (name, one-line claim) | 5% | 0:20 to 0:26 |
| Demo | 45 to 55% | 0:26 to 1:25 |
| Proof (number, customer, logos) | 10 to 15% | 1:25 to 1:45 |
| Vision | 5 to 10% | 1:45 to 1:55 |
| Call to action | 3 to 5% | 1:55 to 2:00 |

The demo share fits the one number sourced: Shortcut was over 50% product walkthrough.

## 3. Voice

- Founder to camera dominates on X launches. Source: Flowjam numbers above.
- Narrator or quiet voiceover: Daylight (quiet narrator), Thinking Machines (manifesto-style voiceover, product de-emphasised). https://speedrun.substack.com/p/how-to-make-a-viral-launch-video and https://www.flowjam.com/blog/product-launch-video-examples-2026
- Founder voice only, no face: Mercury's Series C film is narrated by its founder after the team failed to find voice talent that sounded real. https://www.lorikeetcx.ai/news/great-brand-extends-beyond-marketing-heather-mackinnon-mercury
- No talking at all: Claude x Blender demo, about 23 seconds, sped up. https://speedrun.substack.com/p/how-to-make-a-viral-launch-video
- The "raw" founder delivery is usually scripted and rehearsed. https://www.sean-weldon.com/blog/2026-10-25-this-is-how-you-should-actually-make-launch-videos
- Sentence length: not measured in any source. JUDGMENT: short declaratives, 6 to 14 words, one idea each.
- Words per minute: documentary narration is about 130 to 145 wpm; above 160 sounds like an explainer; 150 is the general default. https://bityclips.com/voice/documentary-narrator, https://www.voicebooking.com/en?p=9233, https://typecast.ai/learn/?p=14931. Planning rule: 750 words is about 5 minutes (https://typecast.ai/learn/?p=15068). Text-to-speech defaults sit near 165 wpm, so slow them down (bityclips).

## 4. How the product is shown

- Real screens carry the best launches. Show real product screens and avoid mock-ups after the first 10 seconds (that page is a studio promo, so treat as opinion). https://www.flowjam.com/blog/yc-startup-launch-video-2025-watch-steal-ship-faster
- Cursor-following zoom into a real interface (FLORA). https://speedrun.substack.com/p/how-to-make-a-viral-launch-video
- Uncut real-task demos did well: an agent finishing a task with no cuts reached 77.7M views, and a zero-cut voice demo reached 3.8M on X and 4.6M on Instagram. https://www.flowjam.com/blog/product-launch-video-examples-2026, https://www.sean-weldon.com/blog/2026-10-25-this-is-how-you-should-actually-make-launch-videos
- Before/after side by side (Reve, HeyGen). Same Flowjam examples page.
- Stylised or abstract: Perigon used abstract motion graphics (30 s). Thinking Machines kept the product abstract. Same page.
- Brand films often hide the product: Mercury's content is "product-adjacent" with no demo (https://zelios.agency/mercury-brand-video-strategy/); Rippling's "One Place" article describes no screens (shots.net link above); Jira is never shown in Atlassian's spot (https://www.moonb.io/blog/best-saas-video-ads).
- Warning: dense UIs need to be shown in one frame, and teams over-focus on aesthetics. https://www.motiontheagency.com/blog/how-startups-use-demo-videos

## 5. Music, sound, pacing

- Music guidance is thin. One YC-format page says use none (vendor opinion): https://www.flowjam.com/blog/yc-startup-launch-video-2025-watch-steal-ship-faster. Mercury's film credits a sound designer, two composers and a separate mix house: https://motionographer.com/quickie/scholar-mercury-banking-should-do-more. Rippling's film credits composers plus a separate sound-design credit: shots.net link above.
- Poor audio makes viewers leave immediately, whatever the picture. https://www.sean-weldon.com/blog/2026-10-25-this-is-how-you-should-actually-make-launch-videos
- Pacing: keep forward narrative momentum rather than editing flourishes or frequent cuts; the first three seconds are the main filter (same Sean Weldon page).
- Cuts per minute: no current source gives a figure. A 1990s study put average commercial shot length under 2 seconds (https://www.warc.com/Content/421e1376-baa5-472f-aa9d-adc58bea5146). JUDGMENT for this film: 12 to 20 cuts per minute in narrative sections; hold demo shots 8 to 20 s; allow one or two long single takes.
- Sync: the 3 to 7 min format lets music rise and fall in chapters. JUDGMENT.

## 6. What makes the best ones feel cinematic

Sourced signals:
- A story device, not a feature list: a Rube Goldberg set with real practical effects and a "Fantastic Mr. Fox" feel (Modern Treasury: https://moderntreasury.com/journal/the-making-of-our-first-brand-video); hand-painted textures scanned and animated, projected onto forms (Mercury: lorikeet and motionographer links); a continuous globe-trotting journey with a single confident character (Rippling: shots.net link); a romantic-drama staging of a software breakup (https://shots.net/news/view/rippling-deliver-because-hr-deserves-better-campaign, seen in search results only).
- Credited craft: sound designers, composers, a mix house, directors (Mercury, Rippling credits above).
- Restraint: "slightly under-explained on purpose" tone. https://zelios.agency/mercury-brand-video-strategy/
- Start from a feeling or identity, not a pain point. Same page.
- JUDGMENT: one visual rule (one palette, one lens look, one texture), motivated camera moves, silence before the reveal, a real voice.

Counter-view, worth taking seriously for a CEO audience: polish is not the goal; belief is. Shortcut's founder said viewers should think the company will make money, not ask who made the video. https://speedrun.substack.com/p/how-to-make-a-viral-launch-video

## 7. 3 to 7 minutes versus 60 to 120 seconds

Sourced:
- YC-format guidance is about 3 minutes or less. https://www.flowjam.com/blog/yc-startup-launch-video-2025-watch-steal-ship-faster
- Long brand films use chapters. A BUCK film for GitHub runs four chapters mixing live action and several animation styles. https://www.columnfivemedia.com/how-7-brands-tell-brand-story (seen in search summary; not fully read)
- Software has no story of its own, so give the narrative to a person; the brand is the mentor, the user is the hero. https://advids.co/insights/the-role-of-cinematic-brand-films-in-b2c-saas-marketing-creating-aspiration (search summary)
- Too much aspiration feels disconnected; too much utility becomes a demo (same).
- Cut one hero film into shorter channel versions. https://www.superside.com/blog/enterprise-brands-scale-customer-storytelling and https://www.motiontheagency.com/blog/how-startups-use-demo-videos

JUDGMENT for the client film: at 4 to 5 minutes add (a) a story spine with one question the film answers, (b) 3 chapters with on-screen titles, (c) one recurring human character, (d) a mid-film turn at about 40 to 50% where the tone shifts from problem to possibility, (e) one quiet beat with no voice and no music, (f) a single proof beat with a hard number. Only 13% of B2B demo scripts back claims with a number (https://www.motiontheagency.com/blog/how-startups-use-demo-videos), so a real number stands out.

## 8. What makes these films feel cheap or AI-made

Sourced:
- AI people: lip-sync, facial motion and character consistency are the weak points, most noticeable in human moments. AI as the main attraction hurts. A hook with no payoff fails. Polished AI cannot fix an unclear story. https://www.motiontheagency.com/blog/how-startups-use-demo-videos
- Uncanny faces, hands, robotic gestures, flat tone; consistency drift across scenes; mispronounced brand names in voiceover; formulaic storytelling. Weak AI ads can create a negative halo on the brand. https://www.lemonlight.com/blog/ai-video-mistakes-brands-make/, https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/ai-ads-have-a-new-problem-this-creative-content-expert-says-they-could-make-consumers-question-the-product-itself (both from search summaries, not fully read)
- Mistakes: batch-generating before validating a test shot; skipping human review of script and voiceover (same search results).
- Feature tours; over-polish; ignoring audio; mistaking view counts for success (3M views gave under 7,000 downloads in one case). https://www.flowjam.com/blog/state-of-launch-videos-2026, https://www.sean-weldon.com/blog/2026-10-25-this-is-how-you-should-actually-make-launch-videos
- Tool limits matter for this plan: Seedance 2.0 caps a clip at 15 s, with 4 to 15 s options and multi-shot working best at 12 to 15 s; stitch many clips and chain them with first/last-frame inputs. https://blog.segmind.com/seedance-2-0-video-duration-length-cost-use-cases/. Hailuo 02 is reported at 10 s max. https://dreamina.capcut.com/resource/seedance-vs-hailuo-02 (search summary only; third-party sources, not official specs).
- An AI Kalshi ad (30 s, Veo 3, about $2,000, two days) aired during the NBA Finals, so AI can pass at short length. https://echai.ventures/videos?category=launch

JUDGMENT, specific fixes for an AI-generated film:
- Never hold an AI face in a long close-up with speech. Use hands, backs of heads, silhouettes, over-the-shoulder, objects, spaces.
- Never generate the UI. Screen-record the real portal and composite it. AI cannot render legible, correct data.
- Never generate text, logos or numbers in-frame. Add them in the edit.
- Record a real human voice (or a licensed cloned voice of the sender) rather than default text-to-speech.
- Lock a style bible and reuse reference frames for every shot.
- Cap every AI shot near 4 to 6 s of usable footage; cut on motion.
- Grade and add film grain and sound design across all shots so the clips share one look.
- Do not use one AI "look" (the glossy, overly smooth, slow-pan default) for everything.

## 9. Recommended beat sheet: 4:30 film for the client

Premise. The film is a letter to the CEO. It introduces the firm and the operating system built for it. Audience of one: a senior executive who knows the portfolio better than anyone. So: restrained, specific, true, no hype words. JUDGMENT unless a source is cited.

Spine. One question, "What would it look like if the firm could see everything it already knows?" Three chapters. One recurring human: an operating partner (non-speaking or voiced, never a close-up talking AI face). Voice: one human narrator at about 140 wpm, total about 560 words for 4:30 including pauses (check against https://typecast.ai/learn/?p=15068).

| Time | Beat | What the viewer sees and hears | Source of the move |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:12 | Cold open | Quiet, specific image: a partner on a Sunday night, laptop glow, a stack of emails. One line of voice. No logo. | Hook first; first 3 seconds filter (Sean Weldon link) |
| 0:12 to 0:40 | The problem | What the firm knows lives in many places: calls, decks, spreadsheets, people's heads. Cut between small human moments. Voice names the cost in plain words. | Start from a feeling, then the pain (Zelios link) |
| 0:40 to 0:55 | Title and promise | Firm name, then one line: "One place where the firm's knowledge works for it." Silence for one beat first. | One product, one outcome, one promise (Flowjam) |
| 0:55 to 1:20 | Chapter 1: Who we are | Firm in a few true facts: portfolio, people, how it works. Real footage or documents, treated, not generated faces. | Identity and stakes in the first 10 to 20% (Sean Weldon) |
| 1:20 to 2:50 | Chapter 2: The operating system | Real screen capture, lightly stylised: the portal, a live company view, a question asked and answered, a signal surfacing before a person would find it. 4 to 5 demos, 12 to 20 s each, one outcome per demo. Voice narrates what changes for the human, not features. | Demo is the largest block, >50% product in the best (Shortcut); uncut real task works (Flowjam/Sean Weldon) |
| 2:50 to 3:05 | The turn | Quiet beat. Music drops out. The operating partner closes the laptop on Monday morning, already knowing. | Mid-film tonal shift (JUDGMENT) |
| 3:05 to 3:50 | Chapter 3: Proof | One real number and one real example from the firm's own data, on screen, read by the narrator. No invented figures. | Only 13% of B2B scripts use a hard number (Motion The Agency) |
| 3:50 to 4:15 | Vision | The system compounding: more companies, more context, the firm getting sharper each quarter. Wide, slow, music rises. | Vision beat (structure above) |
| 4:15 to 4:30 | Close and ask | One sentence to the CEO and one clear next step: a walkthrough with the team. Firm mark. Silence. | Ask last (Flowjam YC page) |

Production notes for AI generation (Seedance / MiniMax / any):
- Budget about 40 to 60 shots of 4 to 8 s each. Generate 3 to 5 takes of each and pick one.
- Generate only: environments, textures, abstract data visuals, hands and objects, transitions, establishing shots. Record or composite: UI, text, numbers, logos, faces that speak.
- Voice and music: real narrator; custom or licensed score; a human sound pass on top of native audio (Seedance audio defaults to off; keep prompts short and abstract, per the Segmind page).
- Deliver a master of 4:30 plus a 60 to 90 s cut and a 20 to 30 s teaser (hero-and-cutdown model, Superside link).
- Review rule: no shot with a speaking AI face, no unreadable on-screen text, no shot that looks like a stock AI default.
- Fact rule: every number and company name on screen must come from verified firm data. Do not generate or invent any.

Open questions to settle before writing the script: who narrates (the sender, an actor, or a cloned licensed voice); whether the CEO prefers understatement or ambition; which single metric to show in the proof beat; which portfolio companies the firm will let appear.
