Design principles

Ten rules every concept follows.

Distilled from live homepages of PE-owned peers, category leaders and NYC startups, each tied to the KPI it should move.

01

One primary action above the fold, attached to the input.

Hero = headline + one field + one button (email, zip, or chat prompt). Secondary actions are ghost or text links. For the portal landing this is the inline chat; for PP it is a zip check with Book.

RampMercuryAny Hour Services (Any Hour Group)Vanta Trust Center

KPI · Hero CTA click-through; chat prompts per visit; booked jobs per session

02

Show live, real numbers early; scale beats adjectives.

A stat band or ticker computed from real data (datasets, zips, opportunities, staff, locations) with units and a source line. Never hard-code illustrative stats without labelling them.

RampMercuryShermcoSila ServicesPenta

KPI · Scroll depth past the stat band; time on page; credibility in user tests

03

Show the product, not stock imagery.

Use real UI screenshots in device frames or a working demo (dispatch board, bid board, trust controls). Each OS page's interactive demo is its hero asset.

LinearRampHebbiaVanta Trust Center

KPI · Demo interactions; tour starts; demo-to-contact conversion

04

Put proof at the point of decision.

Rating + review count + license or certification next to the CTA (header for local services, badge row for B2B). Proof must sit beside the action, not on an About page.

Parker & Sons (Wrench Group)Any Hour Services (Any Hour Group)Frank Gay Services (Apex Service Partners)HebbiaVanta Trust Center

KPI · Call/booking conversion; lead-to-opportunity rate

05

Make coverage the first question for physical-service businesses.

Location chip, geo banner, zip check or service-area finder in or next to the hero, wired to real coverage data (pp_zips; CET's six New England states, no NYC).

Any Hour Services (Any Hour Group)Parker & Sons (Wrench Group)Sciens Building SolutionsFrank Gay Services (Apex Service Partners)

KPI · Out-of-area call rate (down); qualified-lead share (up)

06

Productize the service into a named offer.

Give the recurring program a name and a hero (S3, Penta live, BlissBlend, Financial Options). Each portfolio OS page (ServiceOS, GridOS, FirmOS, LabOS, SignalOS, HarborOS) follows this pattern.

Sciens Building SolutionsPentaVuoriAmeresco

KPI · Recurring revenue % of total; membership/contract attach rate; exit multiple

07

Make the platform and M&A story visible.

'Acquisitions' or 'Partner with us' in primary nav, founder stories, brand-count stats. The website becomes a proprietary deal-sourcing channel for add-ons.

ShermcoSciens Building SolutionsSila ServicesCalibre ScientificHarbor (Harbor Global)

KPI · Inbound seller conversations per quarter; share of add-ons sourced proprietarily

08

Give buyers self-serve tools for the jobs they repeat.

Calculators, pricing estimators, Quick Order, order history, documents and certificates, client portals. Put them in the header or utility bar, not in the footer.

Intelligent Technical Solutions (ITS)Fisher Scientific (Thermo Fisher)McMaster-CarrVWR, part of AvantorKraft Kennedy

KPI · Tool completions to leads; reorder rate; support tickets per account (down)

09

Neutral base, one accent; typography carries the brand.

White or off-white (or near-black) canvas, one saturated accent reserved for actions, a tight display face plus a mono for numbers. Colour encodes meaning (status, action), never decoration.

LinearRampHebbiaFGS GlobalOuterknown

KPI · Perceived quality in user tests; CTA salience (heatmaps)

10

Offer an AI assistant grounded in your own data, placed where questions come up.

An 'Ask' panel over the company's verified data and FAQ (zip coverage, certifications, fit and size, opportunities) with example-question chips; label its sources. The shared chat widget is the implementation.

Vanta Trust CenterVuoriAny Hour Services (Any Hour Group)LinearOuterknown

KPI · Questions answered without a human; assisted conversion; returns rate (FH)

The rationale

Why a better website plus an OS raises portfolio value.

Lower-middle-market services businesses are priced on how predictable, recurring and scalable their earnings look to the next buyer. A modern front door and a named operating system move exactly those attributes, through six levers.

01

Conversion

The website is the cheapest growth lever a services business owns. Coverage checks, instant booking and proof beside the button turn the same traffic into more jobs and qualified briefs.

38% vs 59%HVAC inbound call booking rate, average shop vs 25+ technician shops (ServiceTitan benchmark)

02

Recurring revenue

Named offers (memberships, O&M contracts, monitoring subscriptions) move revenue from project to repeatable, which is what buyers pay up for.

5–6x vs 10x+EBITDA multiples for project-heavy vs repeatable-service HVAC businesses (PKF)

03

Cost-to-serve

Self-serve tools (quick order, punchout, portals, calculators) take routine work off reps and service desks and win long-tail share.

+15.6% vs ~2%Grainger digital Endless Assortment growth vs rep-led High-Touch, 2025

04

Retention

Fit tools, replenishment and client portals keep customers longer and cut costly returns.

+5 pts → +25–95%profit lift from five points of retention (Bain via HBR); online apparel returns run 19.3% (NRF)

05

Multiple

Visible, data-backed KPIs let the next buyer underwrite quality, which is where the spread between typical and premium deals sits.

6.8x vs 9.8xtypical vs premium middle-market EBITDA multiples, a ~3-turn spread (Capstone / IMAP)

06

Buy-and-build

One OS is the integration layer every add-on plugs into, and the site is a proprietary sourcing channel for the next one.

~17–18.5xSila (Goldman Sachs Alternatives) and Champions (Blackstone): scaled, systems-led home-services platforms
