Design principles

Ten rules every concept follows.

Drawn from sponsor-owned peers and category leaders, each tied to the KPI it should move.

01

One primary action above the fold, attached to the input.

A headline, one field and one button: a ZIP code, an email or a chat prompt. Everything else is a ghost button or a link.

KPI: Hero click-through, questions asked per visit and booked jobs per session.

02

Show live, real numbers early; scale beats adjectives.

A stat band computed from real data (ZIP codes, opportunities, staff, locations) with units and a source line. Illustrative figures are always labelled.

KPI: Scroll depth past the stat band, time on page and credibility in user tests.

03

Show the product, not stock imagery.

Real interface screenshots in device frames, or a working demo such as a dispatch board, a bid board or trust controls. Each OS page leads with its interactive demo.

KPI: Demo interactions, tour starts and demo-to-contact conversion.

04

Put proof at the point of decision.

Rating, review count and licences sit beside the call to action, never only on an About page.

KPI: Call and booking conversion and lead-to-opportunity rate.

05

Make coverage the first question for physical-service businesses.

A location chip, ZIP check or service-area finder in or next to the hero, wired to real coverage data: the Punctual Pros ZIP-code model and CET's six New England states.

KPI: Out-of-area call rate down and qualified-lead share up.

06

Productize the service into a named offer.

Give the recurring program a name and a page of its own. Each portfolio OS page (ServiceOS, GridOS, FirmOS, LabOS, SignalOS, HarborOS) follows this pattern.

KPI: Recurring share of revenue, membership and contract attach, and exit multiple.

07

Make the company and acquisition story visible.

An Acquisitions or Partner with us link in the main navigation, founder stories and brand counts turn the website into a proprietary sourcing channel for add-ons.

KPI: Inbound seller conversations per quarter and share of add-ons sourced directly.

08

Give buyers self-serve tools for the jobs they repeat.

Calculators, price estimators, quick order, order history, documents and client portals, placed in the header or a utility bar rather than the footer.

KPI: Tool completions to leads, reorder rate and support tickets per account.

09

Neutral base, one accent; typography carries the brand.

An off-white or near-black canvas, one accent for actions and a mono face for numbers. Colour carries meaning, never decoration.

KPI: Perceived quality in user tests and call-to-action salience.

10

Offer an assistant grounded in your own data, placed where questions come up.

An Ask panel over the company's own data and FAQ, with example questions and labelled sources.

KPI: Questions answered without a person, assisted conversion and returns rate.

Source: design references, 26 reference homepages reviewed on October 6, 2026.

The rationale

Why a better website plus an OS raises portfolio value.

Buyers pay for earnings that look predictable, recurring and scalable. A better site and an OS move those through six levers.

Lever 01

Conversion

The website is the cheapest growth lever a services business owns. Coverage checks and instant booking turn the same traffic into more jobs.

38% vs 59%HVAC inbound call booking rate, average shop versus shops with 25+ technicians (ServiceTitan benchmark)
Lever 02

Recurring revenue

Named offers such as memberships, maintenance contracts and monitoring subscriptions move revenue from project to repeatable, which is what buyers pay up for.

5–6x vs 10x+EBITDA multiples for project-heavy versus repeatable-service HVAC businesses (PKF)
Lever 03

Cost to serve

Self-serve tools (quick order, punchout, portals, calculators) take routine work off reps and service desks and win long-tail share.

+15.6% vs ~2%Grainger digital Endless Assortment growth versus rep-led High-Touch, 2025
Lever 04

Retention

Fit tools, replenishment and client portals keep customers longer and cut costly returns.

+5 pts → +25–95%profit lift from five points of retention (Bain via HBR); online apparel returns run 19.3% (NRF)
Lever 05

Multiple

Visible, data-backed KPIs let the next buyer underwrite quality, which is where the spread between typical and premium deals sits.

6.8x vs 9.8xtypical versus premium middle-market EBITDA multiples, a spread of about three turns (Capstone, IMAP)
Lever 06

Buy-and-build

One OS is the integration layer every add-on plugs into, and the site is a proprietary sourcing channel for the next one.

~17–18.5xreported multiples for scaled, systems-led home-services companies (Sila, Champions)
