Value-creation playbook · for Tyler Zachem & the Portfolio Resource Group · Oct 2026

Two states today.
Six-state grid by month 36.

Smith + Howard went from one Atlanta office to 11 locations, nine add-ons and about 4x revenue in 3.5 years. CET starts bigger, with Horton and NuWave already inside, so the plan is about 3x in 36 months. Make MA and CT one company, fill in the other four New England states CET is already licensed in, and move the mix toward recurring O&M work. That mix shift is what earns a higher multiple.

        Design rationale · why this page looks and reads the way it does

        Single-line diagram as the hero

        Electrical engineers read single-line diagrams every day, so the expansion is drawn as feeders and a breaker: energized MA and CT nodes and planned RI, NH, ME and VT feeders. The open breaker shows that anything beyond New England is outside the plan. It tells the CET team the plan is written in their language, and shows a lender in one glance that the numbers are New England only.

        Stripe / Ramp product pages

        Borrowed: a KPI-first hero and one accent color (voltage amber) on a calm navy-and-white canvas. The four numbers Tyler gets asked about (revenue, EBITDA, people, sites) are on the first screen, so the page works as a 30-second read before an IC or lender call.

        The Smith + Howard exit release

        Borrowed: the dated "entry → exit" framing. Mapping each CET move onto a playbook that has already produced a Broad Sky exit makes the plan easier for the PRG, lenders and a future buyer to believe. KPI moved: time to an IC-ready plan.

        Peer platforms (IES Holdings, NEC Group / Pfingsten, Crete United)

        Borrowed: the capability-segment story (IES shows its data-center communications line separately) and a partner network where founders keep their brands. The levers and add-on sections show a buyer a growing recurring mix and a repeatable add-on cadence. KPIs moved: recurring share, add-ons closed per year, exit multiple.
